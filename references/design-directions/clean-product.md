# Direction: Clean Product

Mainstream warmth without the slop. This is the hardest direction because it lives
nearest to the AI-default look; it works by making the small decisions the defaults
never make. Reach for it for SaaS, e-commerce, local services, and broad consumer
audiences where "distinctive" must not mean "weird."

## Feel
Trustworthy, light, effortless. A visitor's grandmother and their CTO both feel it was
made for them. Personality comes from type warmth, one confident color, and copy -
not from decoration.

## Type
A humanist or geometric sans with genuine personality for display (Bricolage
Grotesque, Sora, Onest, General Sans-class) paired with a quieter text face (Inter,
Public Sans, Figtree). The pairing rule is what separates this from slop: two faces,
chosen, with the display face doing something Inter alone cannot. Optical sizes and
real weights (a true 500/600 hierarchy), not faux-bold.

## Palette
Warm neutrals - not pure #FFFFFF/#000000; tint the ground faintly toward the brand
(e.g. #FBFAF9 with #17150F ink). One accent with conviction, drawn from the brand or
audience (teal, terracotta, deep green, warm blue) - explicitly not violet-on-gradient.
Semantic colors tuned to the neutrals. Backgrounds vary by section via neutral steps
and occasional soft accent tints, never gradient washes.

## Shape & layout
One radius (8–12px) applied consistently. Borderless-first: cards are background-step
surfaces, or content sits directly on the page with spacing doing the separation.
Shadows one soft level, used rarely. **Colored left borders on cards are banned.**
The repeated primitive is the **feature row**: alternating text/media rows with real
product imagery, generous vertical rhythm (96–128px between sections), copy on a
readable measure. When content is genuinely three items, use a ruled list, a numbered
sequence, or one row with three inline entries - not three identical cards with icons
on top.

## Motion
Soft and purposeful: 200–250ms ease-out entrances staggered per row (not per element),
micro-interactions on the money moments (button press, add-to-cart, form success),
hover lifts of 2px max. One signature moment (a hero element that assembles once) is
worth more than ambient wiggle everywhere.

## Imagery
Real product shots or honest screenshots in device-true framing; real photos of the
real team/place if the business has one; commissioned-feeling illustration only with a
consistent hand. No plastic AI renders, no stock handshakes. E-commerce: consistent
product photography treatment (same background tone, same crop logic) matters more
than any layout decision.

## Traps
This direction fails by relaxing: the moment "clean" becomes "default," the slop scan
lights up. Audit against the ban list hardest here - especially the hero + three cards
skeleton, icon-above-heading, and Inter-alone. The discipline that keeps it alive:
every page must contain at least two decisions a template would not have made (a type
scale jump, an asymmetric row, an unexpected but on-brand accent placement).
